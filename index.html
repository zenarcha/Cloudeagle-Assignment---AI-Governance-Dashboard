Framing

User Persona

IT
IT Operations Manager
Primary persona · SaaS & AI operations

Responsible for AI/SaaS access, usage policies, and organizational limits. Works with Security, Finance, Procurement, and team leads when intervention is required.

Core jobKeep AI adoption visible, controlled, and within approved boundaries.

Three decisions

1What needs my attention?
  • Unapproved AI tools
  • Usage/spend approaching or exceeding limits
  • Significant changes in adoption or spend
2Why is it happening?
  • Which tool?
  • Which team/users?
  • What changed?
  • Is the activity approved?
3What should I do?
  • Monitor
  • Contact the team
  • Adjust the limit
  • Approve/restrict access
  • Escalate

Current pain points

Shadow AI is invisibleEmployees adopt AI outside SSO and procurement, leaving IT without a complete view.
AI data is fragmentedIdentity, usage, spend, and organizational data live across systems, making reconciliation manual.
Alerts lack context"Copilot exceeded its limit" doesn't explain who or what drove the overage.
Governance is reactiveIT often discovers AI usage or overspend only after the problem has occurred.
Too many signals, no prioritizationDozens of tools and alerts make it difficult to know what actually needs attention.

User needs

VisibilityKnow what AI tools are being used across the organization.
ControlSee usage and spend against approved limits.
Early warningSpot unusual growth or approaching limits before they become problems.
AttributionKnow which teams or users are driving the activity.
GovernanceKnow whether each tool is approved and being used within policy.
ConfidenceUnderstand where the data came from and whether it's reliable enough to act on.

Part 2

API & data source plan

No single system tells the truth about AI usage — expense data misses free-tier shadow tools, and SSO misses anything signed up for outside it. The plan below triangulates four data types and reconciles them into one record per tool.

How I got to this list

This list exists because of the decision in the Framing section above, not the other way around — the persona and their monthly budget call came first; the data sources are just what's needed to answer it.

Desirability
Do IT/Finance actually want this view? — validated by every competitor leading with the same discovery + spend pattern.
Viability
Is triangulating four systems worth the build cost vs. the spend it prevents? — yes, given CloudEagle's own "10–30% savings" positioning.
Feasibility
Can we actually get this data, and how fresh? — this is what the four groups below answer.
  • Sequencing (v1 → v2): ship groups C (vendor usage APIs) and A's SSO logs first — official integrations, fastest to stand up, and cover the two numbers the persona needs most (spend, sanctioned users). CASB/EDR in group A and the corporate-card feed in group B follow in v2 — they need security/finance team sign-off and infra work, not just an API key.
  • Freshness is a choice, not a default: vendor usage APIs (group C) are polled every 15–60 min because the budget-owner decision is time-sensitive — catching an overage this week beats catching it next month. HRIS (group D) only needs a nightly poll — department assignments don't change hour to hour, so paying for faster sync there buys nothing.
  • Privacy flag: CASB/browser-level data (group A) and card-level transaction data (group B) are sensitive by nature — before wiring either in, this would go through a security/legal review, not just an engineering estimate. That review matters more here than at most companies, since handling this exact kind of data responsibly is CloudEagle's product.

A. Identity & access — who's using what

This group answers "is a human actually using this tool, and did we provision it." SSO alone under-counts — it only sees what was set up through it — so it's paired with network-level signals that catch tools employees found on their own.

C. Vendor usage — the "limit" side

This is the highest-signal, lowest-latency group — it comes straight from the AI vendor and reflects consumption before it turns into an invoice line. It's also the group a technical background helps most with, since each vendor's admin API has its own shape.

D. Org context — attributing correctly

Every number above is meaningless for a "by department" rollup (like Chart 3 and the department filter) until it's tied to a person's actual team — that mapping only lives in the HRIS.

SourceExamples & endpointAuthMechanismFields pulled → becomes on screen
HRISWorkday HCM API, BambooHR APIOAuth2Poll nightly
  • worker.department, worker.manager→the "Product & Design · owner: R. Iyer" subtext under the Claude Team row, and the value each row is matched against when the department dropdown filters the table
  • every tool's spend, grouped by worker.department→the six department spend bars in Chart 3 (e.g. Marketing's MTD spend, split sanctioned/shadow)
Reconciliation rule: a tool is sanctioned if it appears in the procurement/contract system (group B) or was provisioned through admin-owned SSO (group A). Anything with active usage from the CASB or a vendor usage API (groups A/C) but no such match is flagged shadow — this single rule is what powers the "Unsanctioned" label and Chart 3, and it's why the plan needs all four groups rather than any one of them alone.
Part 3

Why these three charts

01
AI spend vs. approved limit, org-wide
How is AI spend tracking against the limit?
Shows total AI spend, its month-over-month trajectory, and the approved limit in one view. The projection makes a potential overage visible before month-end, rather than after the budget has already been exceeded.
02
AI adoption: sanctioned vs. shadow
Is AI adoption becoming more or less controlled?
Spend alone cannot show whether shadow AI is a temporary issue or a growing governance problem. Tracking sanctioned and shadow adoption over time reveals the direction of that problem. Here, shadow adoption is growing 3.3× faster than sanctioned adoption, making the trend more important than a point-in-time count.
03
AI spend vs. budget, sanctioned vs. shadow, by department
Where is shadow AI creating the biggest governance gap?
The org-wide view shows that there is a risk; this view shows where it is concentrated. Separating sanctioned and shadow spend by department, while comparing total spend with each department's budget, helps IT Ops identify which teams have the largest ungoverned spend and whether it is within or beyond budget.
