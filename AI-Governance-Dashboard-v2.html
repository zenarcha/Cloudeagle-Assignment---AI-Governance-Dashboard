Framing

User Persona

IT
IT Operations Manager
Primary persona · SaaS & AI operations

Owns AI/SaaS access, usage policies, and limits — loops in Security, Finance, Procurement, and team leads.

Core jobKeep AI adoption visible, controlled, and within approved boundaries.

Three decisions

1What needs my attention?
  • Unapproved AI tools
  • Usage/spend approaching or exceeding limits
  • Significant changes in adoption or spend
2Why is it happening?
  • Which tool?
  • Which team/users?
  • What changed?
  • Is the activity approved?
3What should I do?
  • Monitor
  • Contact the team
  • Adjust the limit
  • Approve/restrict access
  • Escalate

Pain points

Shadow AI is invisibleEmployees adopt AI outside SSO and procurement, leaving IT without a complete view.
AI data is fragmentedIdentity, usage, spend, and organizational data live across systems, making reconciliation manual.
Alerts lack context"Copilot exceeded its limit" doesn't explain who or what drove the overage.
Governance is reactiveIT often discovers AI usage or overspend only after the problem has occurred.
Too many signals, no prioritizationDozens of tools and alerts make it difficult to know what actually needs attention.

User needs

VisibilityKnow what AI tools are being used across the organization.
ControlSee usage and spend against approved limits.
Early warningSpot unusual growth or approaching limits before they become problems.
AttributionKnow which teams or users are driving the activity.
GovernanceKnow whether each tool is approved and being used within policy.
ConfidenceUnderstand where the data came from and whether it's reliable enough to act on.

Part 2

API & data source plan

No single system captures the full AI footprint. SSO shows provisioned access, finance shows purchases, vendor APIs show consumption, and browser/network signals reveal activity outside approved workflows. The plan reconciles these signals into one record per tool.

How I got to this list

This list exists because of the decision in the Framing section above, not the other way around — the persona and their monthly budget call came first; the data sources are just what's needed to answer it.

Desirability
Do IT/Finance actually want this view?
Viability
Does the potential business value justify the integration cost?
Feasibility
Can we actually get this data, and how fresh?
Product Decisions
V1 → V2

V1: Start with SSO + vendor usage APIs + contract data — official integrations, fastest to stand up, and enough to establish the core view of who is using what, current usage/spend, and approved limits.

V2: Add browser extension, CASB/EDR + corporate-card data to expand Shadow AI coverage and capture purchases outside procurement. These require additional infrastructure plus Security/Finance sign-off.

Freshness

Vendor: 15–60 min · SSO: 15 min · CASB/EDR: hourly · Finance: nightly · Contracts: event-driven · HRIS: nightly.

Sync frequency follows decision urgency: usage and access data refresh frequently because they can change quickly, while finance, contracts, and org data refresh less often because those decisions don't require real-time updates.

Privacy

Browser extension, CASB/EDR, and card data require Security/Legal review before integration.

A. Identity & access — who's using what

This group answers: "Who is using which AI tools, and is that access managed?" SSO shows applications and users visible through the organization's identity layer; browser and CASB/SASE signals extend discovery to AI activity outside managed access, including tools adopted without SSO. EDR can provide additional activity evidence where available.

Part 3

Why these three charts

01
AI spend vs. approved limit, org-wide
How is AI spend tracking against the limit?
Shows total AI spend, its month-over-month trajectory, and the approved limit in one view. The projection makes a potential overage visible before month-end, rather than after the budget has already been exceeded.
02
AI adoption: sanctioned vs. shadow
Is AI adoption becoming more or less controlled?
Spend alone cannot show whether shadow AI is a temporary issue or a growing governance problem. Tracking sanctioned and shadow adoption over time reveals the direction of that problem. Here, shadow adoption is growing 3.3× faster than sanctioned adoption, making the trend more important than a point-in-time count.
03
AI spend vs. budget, sanctioned vs. shadow, by department
Where is shadow AI creating the biggest governance gap?
The org-wide view shows that there is a risk; this view shows where it is concentrated. Separating sanctioned and shadow spend by department, while comparing total spend with each department's budget, helps IT Ops identify which teams have the largest ungoverned spend and whether it is within or beyond budget.
